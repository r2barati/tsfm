# Benchmark audit: time-series forecasting and TSFMs

**Cutoff: 27 September 2026.** Counts can shift across repository versions. Numbers below identify a cited paper/release, not every leaderboard currently using the name.

## Benchmarks

| Benchmark | Scale and task | Metrics / evaluation | Leakage and OOD | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GIFT-Eval** | Paper reports 23 datasets, about 144K series, 7 domains, 10 frequencies, and around 157–177M observations depending paper version/abstract; 8 multivariate datasets. Separate 230B-point pretraining resource. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10393), [repo](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/gift-eval) | Short/medium/long horizons; univariate and multivariate; point/probabilistic MASE, CRPS/WQL. Some flattened-univariate mode for model compatibility. | Explicit pretrain/test separation is valuable. Paper compares original Moirai with a retrained non-leaking variant and finds leakage improves scores, more at long horizons. This is direct evidence that “zero-shot” is not enough. [Paper appendix](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=Z2cMOOANFX) | Broad coverage but many public legacy datasets; exact aggregate depends on version/task slice. Pin manifests and report per-domain results. |
| **fev-bench** | 100 forecasting tasks across 7 domains; 46 with covariates. 2025 benchmark/library. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26468), [library](https://github.com/autogluon/fev) | Task skill scores and win rates; bootstrap confidence intervals; covariate-aware evaluation. | Greater task diversity; “fresh” does not guarantee no overlap. Audit each model's corpus and exact checkpoint. | Bootstrap uncertainty is a strength; task weighting still matters. Show covariate subset and task-level scores. |
| **TIME** | 50 fresh datasets and 98 tasks with human-in-the-loop screening and operationally chosen horizons. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12147), [leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/Real-TSF/TIME-leaderboard) | Zero-shot point/probabilistic results, pattern-level diagnostics, normalized MASE/CRPS, forecast inspection. | Designed to reduce direct benchmark leakage. Newness does not rule out public exposure after release or transfer of global patterns; record model training cutoff. | Pattern-level view improves diagnostics; curation remains finite and subjective. Pin task manifest and leaderboard date. |
| **Chronos Benchmark II** | 27 datasets declared unseen relative to original Chronos training. [Chronos paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815) | Zero-shot results against statistical/deep models; scaled point/probabilistic metrics; varied domains and mostly univariate tasks. | Clean relative to original Chronos corpus, not necessarily later models. Public reuse makes it historical, not evergreen OOD. | Useful comparison set, limited native multivariate/covariate coverage and vulnerable to later pretraining overlap. |
| **BOOM** | 2,807 production observability series and about 350M observations; BOOMlet is smaller. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14766), [repo](https://github.com/DataDog/toto) | Multivariate telemetry; CRPS/MASE and task-specific scaled metrics. | Private Datadog telemetry provides domain realism. Toto is trained on related proprietary telemetry, so BOOM is not a clean unseen-domain benchmark for Toto. Check split and query overlap. | Hard-to-reproduce private data and training-domain advantage. Strong for observability use cases, not generic TSFM claims. |
| **Monash archive** | 30+ public datasets in archive lineage, thousands to 100K series in competition sets; several frequencies and selected multivariate collections. [Archive](https://forecastingdata.org/), [NeurIPS D&B paper](https://openreview.net/references/pdf?id=wDPsjZNCW) | Fixed competition splits and baselines; classical/scaled metrics. Mostly univariate with varying dataset formats. | Public datasets likely occur in pretraining corpora. Original archive was not designed as a contamination-proof FM OOD test. | Valuable classical baseline and short-series test; current archive count changes. Fixed splits do not test prospective drift. |
| **LTSF / Time-Series-Library** | Public ETT, Electricity, Traffic, Weather, ILI and related multivariate sets; common horizons 96/192/336/720. [LTSF-Linear](https://github.com/cure-lab/LTSF-Linear), [TSLib](https://github.com/thuml/Time-Series-Library) | Mainly supervised multivariate point forecasts with MSE/MAE. | Public and heavily reused; not clean zero-shot OOD. | LTSF-Linear found a one-layer linear model could beat several Transformer baselines under its tested setup. This exposed task saturation and comparison sensitivity, not universal Transformer failure. Preprocessing and tuning differ across papers. |
| **Toto result suite** | Original Toto paper evaluates GIFT-Eval, LSF, and BOOM. [NeurIPS paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/48ea942b60e6c52b50ca90e4a40d8ac2-Paper-Conference.pdf) | Zero-shot point/probabilistic forecast scores. | GIFT overlap is discussed; BOOM is private and training-domain adjacent. | Strong open model release; interpret per benchmark slice and provenance. |
| **TempusBench** | 2026 proposed framework with new data, property-centered tasks, standardized tuning, visual diagnostics. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11529), [code](https://github.com/Smlcrm/TempusBench) | Designed to evaluate seasonality, non-stationarity, and common tuning of classical and pretrained models. | Claims new datasets absent from known TSFM corpora; opaque corpora make this hard to prove. | Emerging framework at cutoff, not yet an established community standard. Tuning-budget parity is important. |
| **TS-Arena** | KDD 2026 Datasets & Benchmarks Track. Live platform covers 186 grid series from multiple operators in 14 challenge definitions; energy horizons/frequencies per platform. [Platform/paper](https://ts-arena.live/about), [technical report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20761) | Forecast pre-registration before future values exist; MASE and ELO/rank with uncertainty described by platform. | Strongest defense against direct future-target contamination. Domain is energy; does not by itself test all domains, joint uncertainty, or operational utility. | Prospective evaluation is valuable. Pin active cutoff, collection rules, and model service versions. |
| **Impermanent** | Live prequential benchmark from evolving GitHub Archive series across repositories, activity types, and frequencies. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08707), [repo](https://github.com/TimeCopilot/impermanent) | Sequential MASE/scaled CRPS and champion-style summaries. | Future observations are unavailable when forecasts are submitted, limiting direct overlap and static-test selection. GitHub series are related and domain-specific. | Live rank changes; freeze cutoff, repo cohort, frequency, missingness, and model versions in any claim. |

## Contamination audit protocol

Every result should record: checkpoint training cutoff; known sources and private-data statement; benchmark dataset/version; identity and timestamp overlap; shared shocks (pandemic, macro events, energy crises); whether test/leaderboard results influenced model selection; training splits available to model authors; covariate publication delay; and code/checkpoint hashes. Meyer et al. describe confusion in partition lineage and global-pattern memorization risks even when a paper did not intentionally train on a test set. [Benchmarking Challenges and Requirements](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13654)

Use distinct labels:
- **Direct overlap ruled out:** an auditable disclosed corpus checked against test series and timestamps.
- **Prospective target unknown at forecast time:** a live pre-registration task.
- **Unknown:** opaque or incomplete training corpus. “No known overlap” is not “no leakage.”

## What benchmark rankings can support

- GIFT-Eval: broad cross-domain, multi-frequency and probabilistic evaluation subject to corpus lineage.
- fev-bench: covariate-aware tasks plus bootstrap uncertainty.
- TIME: fresh task formulations and pattern-level diagnostics.
- BOOM: observability telemetry behavior, especially for domain-specialized models.
- Chronos Benchmark II: historical univariate transfer against the Chronos v1 train set.
- Monash/LTSF: classical comparisons and familiar datasets, not clean OOD evidence for current FMs.
- TS-Arena/Impermanent: prospective temporal generalization in selected live domains.
- None measures sequential utility comprehensively. Add policy simulation and realized cost.
